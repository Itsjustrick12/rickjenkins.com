## One Significant Day

If I could use a single day to sum up my recent thoughts and feelings about life since graduating from UMBC, it would be August 9th, 2026. This day brought with it the release of all the feelings and weight that came with finishing my last college assignments, leaving an amazing student organization I helped resurrect, graduating and saying goodbye to friends (some for the foreseeable future), going on a life changing adventure for a month, finding an apartment in 2 weeks, fully moving out of my childhood home, starting my first "big boy" full time job, and nearly running away from one of the people I hold most dear in my life. There wasn't any big planned life event to note about that brought this about, I was just watching a YouTube video.

## Little Things That Make Life Good

While you might expect the video to have belonged to the typical "How to Fix Your Life" or "Why you are miserable" content categorization we all seem to watch, it was actually a pretty unassuming video titled ["82 Little Things That Make Life Good"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7ASoHd_zE4) by the YouTuber Speeed. I've been watching Speed's videos for a while now, and this video seemed pretty run of the mill for the type of content he makes. 

The video first emphasizes the importance of the small joys that make us feel good and put that extra pep in our steps. Then, for roughly 16 minutes, Speeed literally just lists 82 things like "Weekend Matinee Movies" and "Becoming a regular somewhere." The list isn't as boring as you might think it could be, and it provides a fun way to think about some things you may have not experienced in a while that could be fun to do in the near future. 

**If you'll indulge me, and you have 23 minutes, please watch [the video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=s7ASoHd_zE4) from the start to the VERY end**, then come back to read the rest of this post. It's an excellent feel good video to put on your second monitor and watch while you do something else. As you can imagine, that's how I was "watching" it.

![center](blog/KOHF/VideoThumbnail.png)

## An Unexpected Song

What the video doesn't tell you is that by the time you reach little thing #82, 18 and a half minutes into the video, you've actually been hearing the slow strumming of an acoustic guitar since little thing #77 40 seconds ago. But it's just not possible for you to have noticed the guitar when it started, because you tuned out the video 9 minutes ago. And now the lyrics are coming in.

Hearing the single word "allowance" mentally transported me back to the times when my parents would take Will, Jamie, and I down the street to the Toys R Us in Ventura, California. In that store, we'd each get to pick out one small LEGO set or toy, or choose to pool our sets into one bigger one to share. At the time, that young Rick at that Toys R Us wasn't thinking about anything but the brightly colored yellow boxes on the shelves in front of him. 

![center|A photo me, my mom, my siblings, and our brand new dog Oreo. The photo was taken in January of 2023, 6 months before we moved to Maryland.](/blog/KOHF/CaliforniaWithOreo.jpg)

Even at 22 years of age, I could picture exactly where the LEGO section was in that Toys R Us. They were right past the Fabio Schwartz bins with the funny Monkey and other animal plushies, where else would they be? Though that Toys R Us likely isn't standing today, the memory of those sweet moments spent with my siblings and parents hit me with the weight of every brick that was used to build that store that no longer exists.


![center|An actual picture of my childhood Ventura Toys R Us Lego Section, taken the day the store permanently closed for business in June of 2018.](/blog/KOHF/VenturaToysRUs.webp)


That last line I included above is what broke the tough facade and confidence I had been building over the past two years. I had somehow convinced myself that I was excited about leaving the comfort and amazing environment of university, I was excited to start working full time, and that I was fully ready to live in a city an hour and fifteen minutes away from some of my closest friends and family.

I am grown enough to admit that shortly after coming back to the present from the mental Toys R Us, I started bawling at my desk in my completely empty, bare, white-walled apartment I just moved into. As much as I felt completely alone in that moment, I also felt so unbelievably seen and understood by the lyrics I was hearing. There was something so cathartic about how the song pierced through my attempt to forget my present stressful circumstances playing some game I've played a million times before while having some seemingly nothing burger video fill the background noise.

## A Growing Appreciation

I've never had a song hit me as hard as "King of Having Fun" did that day. While the official track on Spotify doesn't hit quite the same way as it does as the acoustic version performed in the Speeed video, I've found myself revisiting the song (and the full length album of the same name) at least once a week for the past two and a half months. 

In the song, Nick sings about his difficulties with substance abuse / self medicating, recovery, and feelings of numbness and loneliness. The song doesn't expect you to be a recovering drug addict to understand that the time we spend escaping reality often leaves us feeling empty and numb, creating a negative feedback loop in our lives. While I've never been one to resort to substances to numb myself from the troubles in the world, I've definitely found myself using other methods that considered more socially acceptable like social media and video games, as many of us do. The lyrics in this song regarding the topics of presence and escapisim hit hard, regardless of the self image you may have for yourself. This is just one example of how the strong themes in the song made it one of the most heartfelt and complete listening experiences I've had in recent years.

It is for the reasons listed above that my girlfriend Sofie and I found ourselves at Medium Build's concert on the evening of Saturday, October 10th, 2026 in Boston, Massachusetts, 414 miles from my apartment. We hadn't planned our trip to Boston around the concert, it just happened to be an amazing coincidence that Medium Build's tour lined up perfectly with the dates we were planning to be in the city. We were originally going to go to a Celtics game that was 3x the cost of the tickets to the concert, and I am so grateful we pivoted our plans, for both my wallet and ability to tell you about one of my favorite experiences with live music.

![center|A picture of Sofie and I taken in the Boston Metro after a full day of adventuring and coming back from the KOHF concert](/blog/KOHF/Concert.JPG)

If you couldn't guess, the concert was INCREDIBLE, as Nick possesses an amazing stage presence, vocal range, and intensity you won't find in many other artists in his genres. I've been to amazing concerts before, but none that have put the lead performer's emotion and vulnerability on full display like this one did. The concert (and more specifically the album) featured a set list that had me jumping up and down, having a great time, AND full on messy crying in a massive group of people who were all experiencing the same tender moments. I imagine that's why I found myself with streaks down my cheeks and a massive smile on my face by the end of the show.

At the concert, I bought the physical CD for the album, and I've been playing it on my mom's big bulky CD player whenever I clean my apartment or play board games with Sofie. We've both grown to love every song on the album, but my personal favorites would have to be (in no particular order): Do Something Productive, Armor, Feed the Boys, Cone Off, and of course, King of Having Fun. That's not to say the other songs aren't good. I would argue that there isn't a bad song on the album, and the concert made me appreciate each and every one that little bit more.

## Closing

To come full circle on all this, if you watched the Speeed video I linked above, you may have seen that he lists "Listening to an album the entire way through" as one of the little things that make life good. If you have 41 minutes, and you've been feeling overwhelmed by life or recent feelings about anything, I implore you to listen to the full [King of Having Fun Album](https://open.spotify.com/album/6qvLeDfqPRk1w73Vh885S3?si=4Cpr7SUgR0-VHvyGIq7OeQ) when you find your next moment to yourself. The songs are 100% targeted towards men in their 20s trying to figure out their place in the world, but I believe that there is at least one song on here that will at least tug on your heartstrings, regardless of who you are or what you've got going on in life right now.

If you read this whole post, watched the Speeed video, or listened to the album at all, thank you. This became a surprisingly emotional effort for me putting this post together. I found myself bawling once again revisiting the acoustic performance in the Speed video that first touched my heart. I tried to be fully authentic in telling you about my experience discovering this artist to show you a part of myself I haven't made time to tell many people about. I promise most of the other posts on my website won't be nearly this long or vulnerable, but know I deeply appreciate your time! 

Now, go back to doing whatever you are trying to distract yourself from by reading this. Check in with yourself first, allow yourself time to feel anything you've been numbing out, and prioritize your enjoyment of life in all that you do. Thanks again for your time!
