# Highs and Lows in Hokkaido

*In this write up, I write about some of my favorite places and quirky experiences from my trip. I wrote a lot here for the different places I went, mainly to provide context for all the pictures you'll see. If you'd like to just see the pictures, keep scrolling.*

## A Gift to Myself

On the morning of my 22nd Birthday, I hopped on a local flight from Hiroshima's local airport on a trip to Sapporo, the capital city of the northernmost island of Japan, Hokkaido. The island is known for being considerably colder than the other islands in Japan. In the late spring and summer, Hokkaido offered a respite from the increasingly muggy temperatures of my first two weeks in Japan in 2026.

When I booked the flight to Hokkaido on my birthday, I did so with the mentality of giving myself the gift of "solo adventure and discovery." It just so happened that a major weekend long dance festival was set to occur shortly after I planned to arrive in Sapporo, and I figured it would be a great opportunity to experience a unique regional experience I wouldn't be able to see elsewhere during my trip.


## Sad in the City

Going to Sapporo for my birthday was cool and all, but it also meant that I gave myself the gift of being completely alone in a new city 6,234 miles from my home and friends. I found myself off to a rough start in the city, walking through the city streets sopping wet from the heavy rain from the train station to Odori park, where I booked my hotel. It was on this day that I probably felt the most isolated during my entire month long trip to Japan. I hadn't made any connections yet, and I couldn't celebrate my birthday with anyone in person. I was also battling the fact that I had 2 whole weeks before I'd be able to see those I love and sleep in my own bed.

Despite all that, I did my best to get myself up and moving after sulking in my BEAUTIFUL hotel room that I booked with my Chase points I had been saving up. ## Daytrips From Sapporo

Up first, I went to the stunning port town of Otaru, where they specialize in glass, music boxes, and most of all, WHIMSY. Otaru was so fun as a solo traveler, just looking into fun stores down Sakaimachi Street. There were music box stores, glass "outlets," cute cafes, and stores for Sofie's favorite little guys Miffy and Snoopy. It's hard not to love Otaru, it's just so damn cute. The canal is supposed to be the main attraction, but it's honestly really underwhelming if you've ever seen a bigger one.

A couple days later, I took a local express down to Noboribetsu, a volcanic hotspring town nestled in the mountains of South West Hokkaido. There, I did a quick hike up to the massive "hell valley" volcanic region, and over to the Oyunuma Sulfur Pond. Both sites featured stunning views as you'll see below, but my favorite part of hiking around was the sulfur footbath tucked away into the trees a short walk down the road from the Oyunuma Pond. I spent like 30 minutes reading on the kindle I brought with me. It was nice to rest my feet in the warm runoff water coming down from the pond. After making it back to the main city, I spent an hour or so talking to a wonderful older woman in one of the giftshops in my best Japanese I could manage. It was really fun to push my language skills to their unfortunate max talking about Golden Kamui and the local onsen. 

Taking a recommendation from my new friend Maho San, I went up to the ritzy Dai-ichi Takimotokan onsen that feautred over 35 pools that would satisfy my desire to bathe completely naked with random Japanese men and the occasional tourist for the second time during my trip. Honestly, the idea of going to an Onsen is pretty wild outside looking in, but it's honestly the best after a long week of constant movement and travel. During my 2-ish hours in the pools, in one of the ones outside, me and like 6 tubby Japanese guys I was near sat completely silent as a group of deer descended the mounatin and ate the grass just outside the bathing area. It was so incredibly serene it made me forget that I was sharing a space butt ass naked with 6 other butt ass naked guys. Obviously I don't have pictures, so I hope that description helps you understand the appeal if you're like "wow! that's gay!"

My final day trip was out to the Jozankei Onsen town, where I planned to go to another public bathhouse. I ended up waking up super late, and catching a bus super late in the day. I ended up missing the onsen entrance time, but I had a nice time walking around the town and enjoying the nature and beautiful weather. I also climbed a massive rock, pretty cool time.

## The Highlight of My Entire Trip

Leaving Sapporo, I found myself on a long local express headed to Hakodate, the southernmost city on Hokkaido. The city acts as the bridge city back to mainland Japan via the Shinkansen. After rushing onto the train, I sat myself down in my assigned seat next to an older Japanese woman. After catching my breath I greeted the woman next to me, and got hit with that instant polite "Nihongo jouzu" all foreigners get. To challenge myself to use the Japanese fluency I spent my last two semesters in college learning, I tried my best to ask the woman what brought her to Sapporo, and where she was off to. In Japanese, she explained to me she went to Sapporo to see Nagoya's Baseball Team, the Chunichi Dragons, play a game against the Hokkaido Nippon-Ham Fighters, arguably one of the best teams in Japan I would come to learn. I told the woman that I watched Hiroshima's team, The Carp, play the Fighters when I was in the city the week prior. Then I asked "are the dragons good? will they win?" She just laughed her ass off and said "Nai, Nai! Yowai yo!" which roughly translated to me as "no way! they're shit!" She then showed me a baseball leaderboard that showed the Carp as 2nd Worst, and the Dragons as the worst team in the league they played in. It was so funny to laugh about the teams we watched being so terrible at baseball in a language I hardly spoke.

We ended up talking for a major portion of the ride down to Hakodate, with my rough Japanese and sprinkled use of Google Translate for stuff I couldn't quite understand / say. The woman, Kato San, ended up offering me a full sushi lunch, and local treats because I didn't bring any food with me onto the train because I was in such a rush getting to the train. Talking with Kato San was probably the highlight of my entire trip to Japan, and I'll never forget what she said about the Dragons' mascots dancing on their social media page. You'll have to go see the screenshot below LMAO. It's remarkable how much you can discuss with someone who speaks another language using simple language skills and trusty google translate. I have Kato San on Line (Japan's version of WhatsApp), and every now and then she'll send me a picture of her and her friends at a local Dragons game. It's the sweetest thing ever.

## Homies in Hakodate

After I made it to Hakodate, I met up with James, a young guy I met back in Hiroshima. James is about my age, and he worked in the DC area prior to quitting his job and going on a big adventure in Japan. Off rip, I thought it was super cool how we were both from the same region of the world at similar transition periods in life meeting up in a country neither of us lived in.

We had coordinated meeting again to explore Hakodate as I made my way back down to Tokyo on the trains and he made his way up to Sapporo. It was so cool to meet up with him and check out the sleepy city walking around and talking about our lives and all the things we had seen on our respective trips. He's a wonderfully down to earth guy, and he's someone I'll make sure to reach out to if I ever find myself looking for a travel buddy in the future.

After James and I had breakfast at the local fish market the next day and parted ways, I spent my day exploring the more busy side of Hakodate by myself. I ate plenty of little treats and found myself stopping to read in all kinds of pretty spots, from little benches on the sidewalks, benches near the ports, and finally atop Mount Hakodate, where I spent 4 hours trying to power through the book I was reading waiting for the sun to set.
