## Witnessing Community in Hiroshima

This is the second album from my month long trip to Japan in June of 2026. The pictures you will see below come after a run and gun week spent in the Kantō region with my sister Jamie. She left midday on June 2nd, and I was set to begin my multi-week solo journey in Japan. The goal was to dive deeper into cities in different regions I had yet to visit. 

What the pictures may not show well is that I BOOKED IT out of Tokyo the same night that Jamie was off to the airport. This was due to a massive Typhoon making its way east towards Tokyo, threatening to shut down the Shinkansen (bullet train) lines. If you've heard me talk about my trip to Japan in 2023, you'd know we were completely upended by a typhoon, and we lost several days in Kyoto as a result.

To save myself a rough start to my solo adventure, I decided to leave for Hiroshima a day earlier, catching one of the last Shinkansen heading in the western direction. Roughly 4 hours later, I was welcomed to Hiroshima with full on downpour, and one last chance to catch a cable car towards my dinky Airbnb. While the city wasn't very welcoming at first, I came to love it as I spent quiet time wandering the city and pretending as if I was a local.

Hiroshima is most famously known as the first city to ever be struck by an atomic bomb. This tragic event resulted in the death of somewhere between 90,000 to 166,000 people following the initial bombing. The horrors of this event hold a shadow over the city that is evident walking through the serene, yet solemn Memorial Peace Park. The park features the Peace Memorial Museum, which had me reading every item description, watching every video, and feeling immense sadness for every story cut short on the plaques. If you ever have an opportunity to visit western Japan, I encourage you to go. The museum will knock you on your ass and it will have you sitting there for a while. Despite the unforgettable events that befell the city, the citizens of Hiroshima found a way to rise above the tragedy and form an unbreakable community that welcomes all.

I have a lot of love for Hiroshima, as it showcases an awe-inspiring story of community revival. From excellent community dining spots to the packed baseball field for the city's terrible baseball team, you can immediately see the incredible pride the citizens of Hiroshima have for their city and community. While doing some souvenir shopping, I was able to witness the Tōkasan Yukata Festival, which represents the official start of summer in Hiroshima, by pure chance! It was so fun to see all the vendors and families interacting with the stalls and food spots. 

I took my time in Hiroshima, not doing a whole lot for several days but eating out and wandering the blocks. The one day trip I took from the city was out to Miyajima, an island covered with shrines and deer, just like Nara near Osaka. On the way to Miyajima, I met a group of travelers I would end up exploring the island with. One of these travelers was James, a fellow DC area resident from the US! James and I would later end up meeting up in the northern region of Hokkaido a week or so later. It's really crazy how quickly you can make fast friends and build community while traveling, and I recommend solo traveling for this reason! It's not all sunshine and rainbows, but there's nothing like being invited by/inviting a stranger to some kind of experience you both have never done before.

While I don't have too many pictures of all the people I met, I can tell you that Hiroshima and its surrounding areas are some of the most friendly I witnessed in all my time in Japan. Don't skip it if you're in the area!
