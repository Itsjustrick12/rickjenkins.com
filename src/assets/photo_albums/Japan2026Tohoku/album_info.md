One of my goals for my month long trip to Japan included making the overland journey from Sapporo down to Tokyo using the different Shinkansen and jumping off for a day or two at different spots in Tohoku. This ended up turning into two nights in Aomori, four nights in Sendai, then finally, 2 nights in Tokyo before leaving back for the United States. During this time, I made two traveler friends and had some fun cooling down from all my experiences during some calm, light exploration days.

After hopping off the Shinkansen from Hakodate, I ended up in Aomori. After dropping my stuff off at my hotel, I took a stroll to the station area and immediately met CJ. CJ is a PROPER bloke who hails from  Gloucestershire, England. If you didn't know, that's the town where the guys fling themselves down a steep ass hill trying to be the first to grab a wheel of cheese. He embodied all of that energy.

After we met, having a "woah! another white guy!" moment, CJ told me about his goal to ride his bike down from Wakkanai (the northernmost city in Japan) down to Cape Soya (the southernmost point in Japan). He was a real David Goggins type guy, and he was a blast to hang out with. We ended up going to a barber together, and then going to the sick Nebuta float museum, which hosts massive parade floats from major festivals held each year in Aomori. We had some dinner then parted ways, but we kept in contact for the remainder of my trip and almost met back up in Tokyo. Super fun guy.

The following day, I spent my entire morning and afternoon making my way out to a museum to check out a special 70-year exhibit for Miffy, one of Sofie and my favorite little guys. I personally love Miffy's friend Boris Bear. He's just the best. I wasn't allowed to take pictures but god it was so fun learning about how the creator of the Miffy world, Dick Bruna, created his children's books with paper cutouts and negative space to test out which of his iconic colors to use for each character's outfits and backgrounds. Unfortunately, it was a temporary exhibit, but it made me appreciate the little guys so much more. I also spent like $120 on trinkets for Sofie.

After I left Aomori, I found myself in Sendai. Sendai, to be honest, is a relatively weak city compared to most of the places I had been. They specialize in fun food like beef tongue and edamame flavored sweets, but the city itself is not terribly interesting otherwise. However, I did meet a super cool Canadian guy named Felix who spoke Japanese much better than I could, and we ended up spending two days together, having a blast. We went out to Yamadera (the 1000-step climb temple) and out to Yamagata City. The seasonal fruits of Pears and Cherries were wonderful! Felix was so fun to talk to, and we finished our adventures together with a great meal at a yakitori eel restaurant, which served AMAZING fried chicken and buttered eel skewers. I hope to one day visit Felix up in his hometown of Montreal next time I find myself headed north!

Finally, I made the last leg of my trip back to Tokyo. I was only there for a day in a half, but I managed to meet one hell of a character in the form of Naru. After he approached me in a random FamilyMart in Jimbocho, he told me his life story that started with the fact that he woke up fully naked in the office building of his job he started the previous day. Crazy stuff. Anyways, we ate some AMAZING curry rice together to sober him up, then I took him to Karaoke where we sang Kendrick Lamar, Panic At The Disco, and Bruno Mars. He passed out shortly after our last song, and I left him to sleep in the room for another hour. He turned out ok don't worry. Regardless, I was happy to have a true Salaryman experience on the final day of my trip. He was also super chill and loves LeBron James, and he was tapped in on the Knicks.

And that was the end of my month long trip to Japan! As much as it's corny to say "it was so life changing," it really did change my perspective on travel, Japan, and what I find interesting about the world. It was an amazing trip that I couldn't have made happen unless I did it mostly alone, and I'll never forget the experiences and funny interactions, especially now that they're mostly cataloged here! Thanks for reading!
